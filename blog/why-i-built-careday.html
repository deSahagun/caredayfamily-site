One appointment, five apps. Why I built CareDay Family

By Alex, founder of CareDay Family
Leer en español

Think about everything one doctor's appointment takes. The date goes in your calendar. You set a reminder so you don't forget it. On the day, you open a maps app to find the office. At the front desk, you dig through your wallet or camera roll for the insurance card. And the questions you meant to ask? They're in a notes app somewhere, or nowhere at all.

That's five apps for one appointment. Now multiply it by everyone you look after: your kids, a parent, a partner, yourself.

Every one of those apps is good at its job. But nothing tied them together around the appointment itself. So I built CareDay Family: one app that does all five, for every appointment, for the whole family.

You waited weeks for this appointment

Here's why it matters. Getting in to see a doctor takes a long time. In the United States, the average wait for a physician appointment in large cities is now 31 days, up 19% since 2022.1 In Canada, the median wait just to see a specialist after your family doctor refers you is 15.3 weeks, almost four months.2

31 days The average wait for a doctor's appointment in 15 large US cities. In Canada, seeing a specialist after a referral takes a median of 15.3 weeks.

After waiting that long, it would be a shame to show up late, without the insurance card, or blank on the one question you really needed answered. And if you miss it, you're often back at the end of the line.

What being ready looks like

I wanted CareDay to walk you through an appointment from the day it's booked to the moment you sit down with the doctor. Here's what that looks like with the default reminders.

1. A calendar for the whole family

Every appointment lives in one list and one month calendar, with a color for each person. Doctor, dentist, therapist, the eye doctor, even the vet. One glance tells you who has what, and when.

2. Reminders with time to prepare

Most reminders arrive when it's too late to do anything about them. CareDay's default is a week, a day and an hour before. The one-week reminder is the one I care about most: it's enough time to ask for the afternoon off, let the school know your kid will be out, or line up a ride.

3. Directions to the office

The address is saved with the appointment, so you never have to search for it again. Swipe the appointment and choose Apple Maps or Google Maps, and you're on your way.

4. A wallet for your documents

When you get there, everything the front desk asks for is in one place: insurance cards front and back, referrals, vaccination records, lab results. They're on your phone, organized by person, and you can lock them behind Face ID.

Need to get a referral to a specialist or a vaccination record to your kid's school? Send any document by Mail, Messages or AirDrop in one tap, with a clear file name like "Leo – Insurance Card," so the person on the other end knows exactly what they're getting.

5. Notes for your questions

Appointments are short, and it's easy to forget what you wanted to ask once you're in the room. Every appointment in CareDay has a notes section. Jot questions down as they come up in the weeks before ("Is this rash normal?", "Should we change the dose?"), then open them when the doctor asks if there's anything else.

For you, or for anyone you care for

Whether the appointment is yours, your child's, your mom's or the dog's, it works the same way. You get the reminder in time to plan, the directions on the day, and the documents and questions in your hand when you walk in. Everything is ready to go.

That's why I built CareDay Family. The wait for an appointment is long enough. Showing up ready shouldn't be the hard part.
